# Level 1 · Sightseeing · Lesson 2: Tickets & Opening Hours (shared spec)

**Goal:** buy tickets or show a booking, ask when a place opens or closes, understand a simple time, and spot "booking required" and the closing day. About 7 minutes.

This spec lists **meanings**, not words. Write each item in the target language as a polite local would say it in the destination country (see SKILL.md, Writing the items).

**Times:** keep spoken times simple: an hour, "half past" (e.g. "at nine", "at half past eight"). Times on signs are digits (often 24-hour: 14:30) and need no words; say once how the country writes them. If the learner hasn't done numbers yet (Shopping · Lesson 1), say the time and show the digits in text, and don't count a missed time as wrong. Minutes, dates and the spoken 24-hour clock are Level 2.

## Speak — what you say

| # | Meaning | When | Target-language note |
| :- | :------ | :--- | :------------------- |
| S1 | "Two tickets, please." | Ticket desk | Any number the learner knows; "one" and "two" are enough. |
| S2 | "I have a reservation." / "I booked online." | Ticket desk or entrance, showing your phone | Teach the shortest common form. |
| S3 | "What time does it open?" (and "close") | Planning your day | Teach "open" as the main form and "close" as its pair. |
| S4 | "Is there an audio guide in English?" | Ticket desk | Use the learner's language if it isn't English. |

## Listen — what locals say back

| # | Meaning | Situation |
| :- | :------ | :-------- |
| L1 | "Have you booked?" | The ticket clerk (answer: S2, or "No" + S1) |
| L2 | "At <hour>." / "At half past <hour>." | Answering S3 |
| L3 | "It's closed today. Tomorrow, yes." | At a closed door or desk |

## Read — what you'll see

| # | Meaning | Where |
| :- | :------ | :---- |
| R1 | Opening hours | Doors and websites |
| R2 | Booking required | Ticket desks, websites |
| R3 | Free entry | Churches, some museums and days |
| R4 | Closed on Mondays (and weekday abbreviations, e.g. Mon–Fri) | Opening-hours signs (use the country's usual closing day; e.g. Italy: _Chiuso il lunedì_, _Lun–Ven_) |

**Local note (one sentence, once):** anything that catches visitors out (e.g. Italy: many museums close on Mondays, and big sights like the Vatican Museums, the Last Supper or the Uffizi often need booking in advance).

## Roleplay — 3 short scenes

1. **Ticket desk:** "At the museum, the clerk greets you + L1." → S2 (showing the phone). Clerk: "Perfect, here you are." → thank you.
2. **No booking:** "Next day, a smaller museum. Clerk: greeting + L1." → "No" + S1. Clerk gives a price → learner: S4. Clerk: "Yes, of course."
3. **Hours:** "Tomorrow you want to come early. Ask when it opens." → S3. Clerk: L2 (e.g. at half past eight) + "but on Monday it's closed" (R4 meaning) → learner: thank you + goodbye.
